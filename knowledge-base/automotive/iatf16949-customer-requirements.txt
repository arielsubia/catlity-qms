IATF 16949 study note: customer-specific requirements

Study reference for the Catlity QMS normative agent.
This note is an original summary of topics an auditor reviews. It is not the official text of IATF 16949 and it does not reproduce the standard.

Automotive customers add their own quality requirements on top of the shared management-system requirements. Those customer-specific requirements can cover submission levels, special characteristics, labeling, change notice, and how a supplier reports a problem. The auditor treats them as requirements of the system, not as a commercial appendix that quality can ignore.

The organization needs a way to identify which customer-specific requirements apply to each customer and site. A matrix or an equivalent list is useful when it points to the process that implements each requirement. The auditor picks one customer and follows one requirement into a procedure, a control plan, or a record. A requirement that appears only in a contract folder and nowhere in the process is a gap.

Changes to a customer-specific requirement are distributed to the people who must apply them. The auditor asks what happened the last time a customer updated a portal manual or a supplier requirement: who reviewed it, what changed in the plant, and how obsolete instructions were removed.

Awareness matters on the line. A team leader should be able to name the customer requirement that changes how their process works, for example a label layout or a reaction when a special characteristic drifts. The quality function does not hold that knowledge alone.

This note does not list a particular customer's manual. The agent should treat a named customer requirement as something to look up in the organization's own records, not as text stored here.
